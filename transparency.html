The trust architecture

Trust should be inspectable.

RIPPLE is designed to show what was claimed, what was checked, who checked it, when it was checked, and what remains unknown. Transparency is not a page we add later; it is part of every mission state.

Our rule

No vague “verified” badge.

A school can exist in a public registry while its current request remains unconfirmed. A company representative can control an email address without proving authority to release equipment. A repairer can submit a wipe report without proving delivery. RIPPLE keeps those claims separate.

Self-attested

A participant made the claim.

Useful as a starting point, but not independent proof.

Source checked

A named source supports part of it.

The source and the limits of that check stay visible.

Evidence reviewed

A required record was examined.

The reviewer, date, decision, and exceptions are preserved.

Accepted outcome

The receiving party confirmed the result.

Impact is counted only after the defined acceptance event.

Claim-specific verification

Different claims require different proof.

Checks expire, sources change, and documents can conflict. RIPPLE records the source and limitation rather than collapsing everything into one reputation score.

Restricted giving ledger

Payment is not impact.

A contribution moves through explicit states. Donors can see whether money was merely pledged, received by the charitable program, allocated to a mission, paid against approved costs, or reconciled after completion.

i

In a real pilot, a qualified charitable partner—not the prototype—would receive funds, approve expenses, and issue any eligible acknowledgment.

  1. 01

    Pledged

    A donor expressed intent. No outcome is claimed.

  2. 02

    Received

    The charitable payment processor confirmed receipt, net of clearly disclosed processing details.

  3. 03

    Allocated

    The contribution is assigned to an approved mission budget or disclosed fallback.

  4. 04

    Expense approved

    An authorized reviewer approves a repairer invoice, part, or logistics cost.

  5. 05

    Paid and reconciled

    Provider payment and the actual mission cost are recorded, including refunds or unused funds.

  6. 06

    Impact receipt issued

    Only after recipient acceptance does the receipt show delivered units and completed evidence.

Chain of custody

Every handoff requires two sides.

One participant cannot silently mark the whole mission complete. Releasing and receiving parties record the same transfer, and differences become visible exceptions.

Privacy by role

Proof without exposing people.

Public transparency should describe the mission, not reveal students, sensitive company records, or security-critical device identifiers.

◉

Public

Mission goal, general location, itemized budget, stage, aggregate device counts, evidence statuses, and accepted outcome.

◐

Participants only

Pickup details, named contacts, invoices, exception photos, repair notes, and organization documents required for the work.

●

Restricted

Serial numbers, asset manifests, authorization records, payment identifiers, security reports, and reviewer-only evidence.

×

Not collected

Student names, home addresses, household income, grades, immigration status, or identifiable beneficiary stories unless legally necessary.
