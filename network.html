The activation gap

A donated laptop is not yet a learning device.

Between a company saying “we have equipment” and a student receiving something safe and useful are the expensive, invisible steps that usually break the chain. RIPPLE makes those steps fundable and traceable.

  1. 01
    Declare

    A company records the equipment, ownership, condition, pickup window, and data-bearing status.

  2. 02
    Accept

    A verified school or charity states the quantity, minimum specification, use, and delivery constraints.

  3. 03
    Scope

    Qualified repair partners inspect the batch and separate reusable, parts-only, and recyclable units.

  4. 04
    Activate

    Donations fund only approved work: secure erasure, parts, labor, packing, delivery, and responsible recycling.

  5. 05
    Prove

    A batch passport records custody and evidence. Impact is counted only when the recipient accepts delivery.

Proof before applause

Every public number should answer: “according to whom, and when?”

RIPPLE does not ask people to trust a feel-good total. Each batch carries a passport with claim-specific evidence, so “offered,” “repairable,” “delivered,” and “accepted” never get blurred into the same number.

  • Chain of custodyWho held the batch at each handoff.
  • Restricted cost ledgerWhat each funded dollar was assigned to.
  • Recipient acceptanceThe final event required before counting impact.
  • Failure visibilityUnits recycled or rejected stay visible, too.
